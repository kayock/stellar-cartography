# Ancestral Puebloan — research dossier

**Display term:** Ancestral Puebloan  
**Search aliases:** Anasazi; Ancestral Pueblo; Ancient Puebloan  

Older archaeological and documentary sources often use “Anasazi.”
Cartography uses **Ancestral Puebloan** as the primary label.
“Anasazi” is never the preferred cultural name.

This tradition is **archaeological**. Cards must say
“Archaeological interpretations suggest…”  
They must **not** say “this is the story the Ancestral Puebloans told.”

Descendant communities include modern Pueblo peoples (and others
with traditional affiliation). This pack does not speak for any
one living pueblo.

## Well-supported public archaeoastronomy

### Fajada Butte Sun Dagger (Chaco Culture NHP) — READY

Three sandstone slabs channel midday sunlight onto spiral
petroglyphs. Sofaer, Zinser, and Sinclair (1979, *Science*)
described solstice and equinox light patterns. NPS states the
site is believed to have marked solstices and equinoxes; visitor
erosion shifted the slabs; the site is closed to the public.

Evidence type: **archaeological_inference** (with a later
preservation caveat). Lunar-standstill claims (Sofaer 1982) are
more contested and are **not** implemented.

Sources:

- NPS, “Archeoastronomy in Stone” (updated 23 Jan 2024).
  https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/archeoastronomy-in-stone.htm
- NPS History collection, Fajada Butte solstice brochure (2022).
  https://npshistory.com/publications/chcu/brochures/fajada-butte-2022.pdf
- Sofaer, Zinser, Sinclair, “A Unique Solar Marking Construct,”
  *Science* 206 (1979).
  https://solsticeproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/19-uniquesolar2028197929.pdf
- HAO / UCAR education note on later slab shift (1989).
  https://www2.hao.ucar.edu/education/prehistoric-southwest/sun-dagger

### Chaco cardinal architecture — READY (cautious)

Pueblo Bonito and related Chaco constructions have documented
near-cardinal wall and axis orientations (Williamson et al.;
Sofaer / Solstice Project surveys; NPS interpretive material).
These are treated as architectural astronomy, not constellations.

Casa Rinconada’s proposed summer-solstice light-through-niche
alignment is **contested** because of 1930s reconstruction
(Exploratorium / NPS interpreters). That specific light effect
is **RESEARCH_MORE**, not this card.

Sources:

- NPS Pueblo Bonito place page.
  https://www.nps.gov/chcu/planyourvisit/pueblo-bonito.htm
- NPS Casa Rinconada place page (mentions solar alignments).
  https://www.nps.gov/places/casa-rinconada.htm
- Solstice Project / Lekson chapter on Chacoan primary
  architecture (cardinal walls, North Road).
- Exploratorium “Ancient Observatories: Casa Rinconada”
  (notes reconstruction uncertainty).
  https://annex.exploratorium.edu/chaco/HTML/rinconada.html

### Holly Petroglyph Panel, Hovenweep NM — READY (deepening pass)

NPS Holly pages state that a summer-solstice beam through a
boulder gap bisects spiral and concentric-circle petroglyphs.
The observable light effect is treated as **STRONG**. Ancestral
intent remains **SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION** (NPS “we believe”).
Hovenweep Castle portholes and lunar claims are not added.

### Other NPS solar markers — RESEARCH_MORE / unused this pass

- Puerco Pueblo, Petrified Forest NP — solstice spiral (NPS).
- Mesa Verde Sun Temple — NPS: possible winter-solstice marker
  as seen from Cliff Palace; “many questions remain.” Fewkes’
  south-wall solstice alignment is **DISPUTED** (Reyman 1977).
  Visual-mode foundation exists; no production story.
- Peñasco Blanco 1054 supernova pictograph — NPS says **may**
  depict the Crab supernova beside a crescent moon. Interpretive.
  **RESEARCH_MORE.**
- 1097 eclipse / Venus petroglyph — NPS “may represent.”
  **RESEARCH_MORE.**

## Continuity

NPS notes that descendant communities find meaning in these
places. That is acknowledged. It is **not** treated as proof that
a living oral constellation story is being retold here.
