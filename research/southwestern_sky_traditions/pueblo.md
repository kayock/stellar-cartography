# Pueblo traditions — research dossier

**Do not create** a generic “Pueblo Sky Mythology” card.

Hopi, Zuni (A:shiwi), Acoma, Taos, and other Pueblos were researched
as separate communities. A story is implemented only if
`source_people` can name one community and the source is public
and appropriate.

## Hopi

Sources reviewed:

- Hopi Cultural Preservation Office (tribal office; NPS Teaching with
  Historic Places points teachers there). Public office exists; no
  downloadable Hopi-authored star-asterism curriculum was obtained
  for this pass.
- Stephen C. McCluskey, “Calendars and Symbolism: Functions of
  Observation in Hopi Astronomy,” *Archaeoastronomy / JHA* (1990).
  Scholarly account of horizon sun-watching and planting/ceremonial
  calendars.
- HAO / UCAR education page on Hopi Tawa Kátsina and sun-priests.
  https://www2.hao.ucar.edu/education/prehistoric-southwest/hopi-tawa-katsina
  Secondary educational summary of horizon observation. Katsina cycle
  is ceremonial.
- University of Chicago eCUIP “Anasazi / Hopi” cultural-astronomy
  pages: generic encyclopedia tone; uses outdated “Anasazi” framing;
  claims Hopi Pleiades “Chuhukon / Seven Sisters.” **Not used** as a
  primary source.
- Wikipedia “Pleiades in folklore” (Hopi Tsöötsöqam / Hotòmqam):
  secondary. Useful only as a lead.
- Bertam Tsavadawa conversation on Hopi astronomy (public talk):
  winter-solstice calendar, Orion as important, and explicit note
  that some knowledge is **not shared**.

Recommendation: Hopi **horizon solar calendar** is well documented
as practice, but tying it to katsina ceremonial timing and to
specific star names from secondary pages is not READY for a
year-round story card. **RESEARCH_MORE / DEFER.**

## Zuni / A:shiwi

- A:shiwi A:wan Museum and Heritage Center (community museum).
  Preferred venue for public Zuni education; no star-asterism
  handout was available for reuse here.
- Mesa Verde Voices S5E4 “Star Watching”: Octavius Seowtewa (Zuni)
  shares that prayers address all glittering stars
  (*Ts’uhaba Moyachu: A:shiwanni*). Not a mapped asterism story.
- Zuni visitor-center / world-view talks mention Father Sun, North
  Star, Morning Star. Too thin, and some content is religious.

Recommendation: **RESEARCH_MORE.** Visit/community-authored text
needed before any Zuni card.

## Taos

UNM-Taos Cielo Centro is a public STEM observatory project, not a
Taos Pueblo sky-tradition source. Do not treat it as Pueblo
astronomy. **DEFER** Taos Pueblo sky stories.

## Pan-Pueblo secondary material

Lowell Observatory Native American Astronomy Outreach is STEM
classroom pairing, not a constellation story source.

Any source that says “the Pueblos believe…” without naming a
community is rejected.

## Three Sisters

No Pueblo-community source reviewed documents a celestial
“Three Sisters.” Agricultural Three Sisters (corn, beans, squash)
is a crop complex, not an asterism, and is not used as a sky story.

Hopi Pleiades rendered as “Seven Sisters” in English secondary
pages is treated as a later English/Greek label, not an authentic
translation, unless a Hopi-authored source says otherwise.

## Implementation recommendation

**No Pueblo living-tradition story this pass.**

## Deepening pass

A:shiwi A:wan *Ulohnanne – The Zuni World* is a community map-art
exhibit that includes sky as part of cosmology, not a named
asterism handout. El Morro NPS hosted a 2019 Zuni constellation
evening with Kenny Bowekaty (Zuni Pueblo Department of Tourism);
that is an event announcement, not reusable star-name text.
Hopi, Acoma, and Taos remain **RESEARCH_MORE / DEFER**. No living
Pueblo oral story was promoted.
